Part 1 · How AI changed

In 2026, AI became a teammate.

Give it a task. It plans, codes and tests. You review.

booking-appAI agent working
$ agent "Let users book by phone number" ● Reading the project… (214 files) ● Plan: 4 steps ✎ Editing booking.js, api.js, form.tsx… ✓ 48 tests passed → Ready for your review
(~1.5 min) Let it type. In 2025 most tools suggested the next line. In 2026 "agents" take a whole task: read the code base, plan, edit many files, run the tests, and hand back a finished change for a person to review. Frame it as exciting: one engineer can now build much more. Ask: "Who has used an AI agent like this?"

Part 2 · What skills matter now?

AI still needs people who understand code.

Your coding skill stays valuable.

4 in 10pieces of AI-written code failed security tests

(~1.5 min) Good news for anyone learning to code: AI is fast, but not reliable on its own. In Veracode's July 2026 test, AI-written code failed security checks about 44% of the time: more than 4 in 10. Someone has to read, judge and fix it, and take responsibility. (Another study, CodeRabbit Dec 2025, found 1.7 times more issues per AI-written change.) Coding skill stays valuable; it just isn't the only thing that counts.

(~1.5 min) A calm way to see it: anything that can be written as steps (manuals, rules, routine code) AI can learn quickly. What is hard to write down grows in value: understanding people, asking the right question, and the trust clients give you. Optional background if asked (keep it neutral): in 2026 some companies asked employees to write their know-how down as "skills" for AI agents (e.g. the viral "colleague.skill" project in China, MIT Technology Review, Apr 2026). What you learn in Japan

Clients don't say everything. Learn to notice.

Japan is a high-context culture. Working there, you learn to sense what clients really want, and deliver it with care.

察するsassuruto sense what is not said
★ Japan Quality Care in every detail
WHAT THEY SAY"Please make a booking app."
WHAT THEY REALLY WANT
Easy for older customers
No mistakes on busy days
Quick help when stuck
(~2 min) AI cannot talk with your clients, and clients rarely say everything they want. Japan is a high-context culture: people expect you to understand what is not said. The Japanese word is 察する, sassuru. Example: the client only says "Please make a booking app." Under the surface they want it to be easy for older customers, to work without mistakes on busy days, and to get quick help when stuck. Working in Japan trains you to notice (気づく), think ahead (先回り) and care about every detail (心配り). Click: that is Japan Quality, service that clients trust and come back for.

(~1.5 min) AI is now on both sides. Defense: in Anthropic's Project Glasswing, about 50 partner organisations found more than 10,000 high or critical vulnerabilities with AI (update, May 2026).
