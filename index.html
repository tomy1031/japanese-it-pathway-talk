Life in Japan

Safe. Even at midnight.

Walk home late and feel relaxed. Safety is one of the top reasons foreigners like living in Japan.

Many students and parents worry about safety abroad. In Japan, people walk home late at night, children ride trains alone, and lost wallets often come back.
Life in Osaka

The kitchen of Japan.

Takoyaki, ramen, okonomiyaki, sushi. Great food that does not cost much.

Osaka has been called "the kitchen of Japan" for centuries. Food is a big part of daily happiness, and in Osaka a good lunch is cheap.
Life in Japan

Four seasons. Endless weekends.

Kyoto, Nara and Kobe are about an hour or less from Osaka by train.

Cherry blossoms in spring, festivals and fireworks in summer, red leaves in autumn, snow in winter. Every season has its own trips, and from Osaka they are close.

What you learn in Japan

Clients don't say everything.

Japan is a high-context culture: much of the message is in what is not said. Working there, you learn to listen, ask and notice.

察するsassuruto sense what is not said
WHAT THEY SAY"Please make a booking app."
WHAT THEY REALLY WANT
Easy for older customers
No mistakes on busy days
Safe, simple payment
Quick help when stuck
Clients rarely say everything they want. In Japan this is even stronger: it is a high-context culture, so people expect you to understand what is not said. The Japanese word is 察する, sassuru: to sense what someone means. Example: the client only says "Please make a booking app." Click four times to show what they really want under the surface. Working with Japanese clients trains you to listen, ask good questions and notice these needs. AI cannot do this for you.

Explain JLPT simply: it is the standard Japanese test, from N5 (easiest) to N1 (hardest). N3 is about everyday conversation; N2 is business level. Our first goal is N3 in one year: that takes steady study every week. Passing N3 lets you work with a Japanese team, and going on to N2 opens even more doors.
